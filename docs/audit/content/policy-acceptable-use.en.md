# Acceptable Use of Information Resources Policy

Rules for the secure and lawful use of the Organisation's accounts, email, internet, devices, data and AI services. Every employee reads the policy and acknowledges it in the training platform with their password.

## 1. Purpose
1.1. This policy sets out the rules for the secure, lawful and appropriate use of the Organisation's information resources.

1.2. The purpose of the policy is to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information, to ensure the protection of personal data, to reduce the risk of cyberattacks and incidents, and to define clearly the duties of every employee in this area.

## 2. Scope
2.1. The policy applies to all employees of the Organisation, including managers, civil servants and interns, and to contractors and other persons who have access to the Organisation's information resources under a contract (hereinafter "employee").

2.2. Work with information constituting a state secret is governed by the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On State Secrets" and the special rules adopted under it. This policy does not replace those rules; it supplements them.

2.3. Where other internal acts of the Organisation set a stricter requirement, the stricter requirement applies.

## 3. Legal basis and standards
3.1. The policy is based on the following legal acts and is applied in accordance with their current wording:
- the Constitution of the Republic of Azerbaijan;
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Information, Informatization and Protection of Information";
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Personal Data";
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Electronic Signature and Electronic Document";
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Obtaining Information";
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Commercial Secrets";
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On State Secrets";
- the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Civil Service" (in respect of civil servants);
- the Labour Code, the Criminal Code and the Code of Administrative Offences of the Republic of Azerbaijan;
- the "Strategy of the Republic of Azerbaijan on Information Security and Cybersecurity for 2023-2027", approved by Order of the President of the Republic of Azerbaijan dated 28 August 2023;
- the "Requirements for the Protection of Personal Data", approved by Decision of the Cabinet of Ministers of the Republic of Azerbaijan.

3.2. The policy takes into account the following standards:
- AZS ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (ISO/IEC 27001:2022) and ISO/IEC 27002:2022, including controls A.5.10 (acceptable use), A.5.15-A.5.18 (access control and authentication), A.5.34 (protection of personal data), A.6.3 (awareness and training), A.6.4 (disciplinary process), A.6.8 (event reporting), A.7.7 (clear desk and clear screen), A.8.1 (user endpoint devices);
- NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, PL-4 "Rules of Behavior";
- NIST SP 800-63B-4 (authentication and passwords);
- CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1, Control 14 (security awareness and skills training).

## 4. Definitions
- **Organisation**: the state body, legal entity or structural unit that has approved this policy;
- **Information security officer**: the person or unit appointed by decision of the head of the Organisation to be responsible for organising information security;
- **IT unit**: the unit of the Organisation responsible for operating information technology, or a contractor providing this service;
- **Information resources**: paper and electronic information owned by the Organisation or processed on its behalf, information systems, software, accounts, networks, cloud services and devices;
- **Personal device**: a computer, tablet or mobile phone owned by the employee and used for work with the Organisation's permission;
- **Personal data**: any information that makes it possible to identify a person directly or indirectly;
- **Confidential information**: information to which access is restricted by law or by decision of the Organisation, including confidential personal data and commercial secrets;
- **Incident**: an event that causes or may cause a breach of the confidentiality, integrity or availability of information, as well as a reasonable suspicion of such an event;
- **Multi-factor authentication**: confirmation of identity by at least two independent factors, for example a password and a one-time code from a mobile app;
- **Password**: a sequence of characters known only to the user and used to access an account, including a passphrase made of several words;
- **Social engineering**: a method of deceiving a person into disclosing information, opening a link, making a payment or breaking a rule, including phishing (a fake email or message), vishing (a fake phone call) and quishing (a fake QR code);
- **Public AI service**: a general-purpose artificial intelligence service that has no contract with the Organisation and processes the data entered into it on the provider's servers.

## 5. General principles
5.1. The Organisation's information resources are provided for the performance of work duties. An employee obtains access only to the information and systems needed for their duties and is personally responsible for actions performed under their account.

5.2. Brief personal use of devices and the internet is allowed only where it does not interfere with work, does not create cost or risk for the Organisation and does not breach this policy.

5.3. An employee leaving the Organisation returns the devices, media, documents and access means issued to them and deletes work data from personal devices and accounts. The obligation not to disclose confidential information remains in force after the employment ends.

## 6. Accounts and authentication
6.1. Each employee is given a personal account. Handing over an account, password, one-time code, access card or electronic signature tool to anyone else, including a manager, a colleague or a member of the IT unit, is prohibited.

6.2. No member of the Organisation's staff asks an employee for a password or one-time code. Such a request is treated as a sign of social engineering and is reported immediately.

6.3. A password:
- consists of at least 12 characters, and of at least 15 characters on systems without multi-factor authentication; a passphrase of several words is recommended;
- does not consist of a name, date of birth, phone number, the Organisation's name, or a common or leaked password such as "123456";
- is different for each system; a work password is not used for personal accounts.

6.4. Passwords are not kept on paper, on notes stuck to the monitor, in unencrypted files or in messages. Only a password manager approved by the Organisation is used to store passwords.

6.5. If it is suspected that a password has become known to someone else or has been entered on a fake page, it is changed immediately and the case is reported as an incident.

6.6. Multi-factor authentication is used on all systems where it is available, first of all for email, remote access, cloud services and administrator accounts. An employee does not approve a sign-in request they did not start and reports it immediately.

6.7. The screen is locked whenever the employee leaves the workplace, even briefly (Windows: Win+L, macOS: Control+Command+Q). Devices lock automatically after no more than 5 minutes of inactivity.

## 7. Email and messaging
7.1. Work correspondence is conducted only through the Organisation's email and approved messaging tools. Forwarding work emails to a personal mailbox is prohibited. The work address is not used to register for services unrelated to work.

7.2. Signs of phishing are:
- pressure through a demand for urgent action, a threat of penalty or a promise of reward;
- a small difference between the sender's address or domain and a familiar one;
- a request for a password, one-time code, bank card or personal data;
- an unexpected attachment, archive file, document with macros or file-sharing link;
- a request to change payment details.

7.3. Before a link is opened, its real address is checked (by hovering the pointer over it, or by pressing and holding it on a mobile device). Sign-in details are entered only on a page whose address was typed by hand or opened from a bookmark approved by the Organisation.

7.4. An unexpected attachment, even from a familiar sender, is not opened until it is confirmed with the sender through another channel. Macros are enabled only with the permission of the IT unit.

7.5. A suspicious email is reported through the reporting button or channel set by the Organisation; it is not answered and not forwarded. If a link has been opened, a file run or data entered by mistake, this is reported immediately.

7.6. Confidential information is sent outside the Organisation only when work requires it and only with an approved encryption tool. Recipient addresses are checked before sending.

## 8. Internet and cloud services
8.1. Software, browser extensions and mobile apps are installed only with the permission of the IT unit and from an approved source. Downloading unlicensed or illegally distributed software and content is prohibited.

8.2. Work data is stored and shared only in cloud services approved by the Organisation. Storing it in personal cloud storage, personal messaging accounts, or online file conversion and transfer services is prohibited.

8.3. Files are shared only with specific persons. Open "anyone with the link" links are not used for confidential information.

8.4. On public Wi-Fi networks, connecting to the Organisation's systems is allowed only through the Organisation's VPN.

8.5. On social networks, only an authorised person speaks on behalf of the Organisation. Confidential information and photos showing internal documents, screens, whiteboards or access cards are not posted. An employee keeps in mind that what they disclose about their position may be used for social engineering.

## 9. Devices
### 9.1. Work computers and mobile devices
9.1.1. An employee does not leave the device issued to them unattended, does not keep it visible in a car, and does not let family members or other outside persons use it.

9.1.2. Disabling or bypassing antivirus, encryption, updates or other security tools is prohibited. Updates are installed without delay.

9.1.3. Repair, disposal or transfer of a device to another person is carried out only through the IT unit, with secure deletion of data.

### 9.2. Personal devices
9.2.1. A personal device is used for work only with the Organisation's permission and on the following conditions:
- the operating system is supported and updated by the manufacturer;
- a screen lock is active and storage is encrypted;
- the manufacturer's restrictions have not been removed ("jailbreak", "root");
- apps are installed only from official app stores;
- where the Organisation requires it, the device is enrolled in the mobile device management system.

9.2.2. On a personal device the Organisation manages only work data and work apps, and may delete them remotely if the device is lost or the employment ends. The Organisation does not access the employee's personal data.

### 9.3. Removable media
9.3.1. Work data is written to a USB drive, external disk or other removable media only when work requires it and only on encrypted media issued by the Organisation.

9.3.2. Media of unknown origin, found or received as a gift is not connected to any device and is handed over to the IT unit.

9.3.3. Work devices are not connected to USB charging ports in public places; only a personal charger or one issued by the Organisation is used.

9.3.4. When no longer needed, information on media is deleted so that it cannot be recovered, or the media is destroyed through the IT unit.

### 9.4. QR codes
9.4.1. After a QR code is scanned, the address it will open is checked. If the address does not match the official domain of the Organisation or of a well-known service, the link is not opened.

9.4.2. No password, one-time code, bank card details or personal data are entered on a page opened from a QR code in an email, on a poster, on a parking meter or in another public place. Suspicious QR codes, such as a sticker placed over the original code, are reported. No software or device profile is installed through a QR code.

## 10. Information classification and protection
10.1. The Organisation's information is divided into the following categories:
- **Public**: information intended for publication or open under the law;
- **Internal use**: information intended for use only within the Organisation, including information for official use under the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Obtaining Information";
- **Confidential**: confidential personal data, commercial secrets and other information whose disclosure may cause serious harm;
- **State secret**: information defined by the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On State Secrets" (clause 2.2 of this policy).

10.2. Information whose category has not been set is treated as "Internal use".

10.3. When working with personal data, an employee:
- collects, processes and keeps it only to the extent needed for their work duties;
- does not give confidential personal data to third parties without the data subject's consent, except in cases provided by law;
- does not copy it to personal devices, personal accounts or public AI services;
- where the law requires it, gives a written undertaking not to disclose it; this undertaking remains in force after the employee leaves.

10.4. Confidential information is disclosed only to persons with access rights and a work need, and is not discussed in public places where outsiders can hear it.

10.5. Where possible, confidential documents are printed with the secure print function and collected from the printer immediately. Documents no longer needed are destroyed in a shredder.

10.6. When working in public places, the employee makes sure the screen cannot be seen by outsiders. Unauthorised photos or screenshots of work screens, documents and whiteboards are not taken.

10.7. At the end of the working day and when away for a long time, confidential documents and media are kept in a locked cabinet. Confidential information is not left on desks, printers, in meeting rooms or on whiteboards.

## 11. Artificial intelligence services
11.1. For work, only AI services approved by the Organisation are used.

11.2. Personal data, information in the "Confidential" and "Internal use" categories, internal documents and correspondence, source code, system configurations, passwords and access keys are not entered into public AI services. Entering information constituting a state secret into any AI service is prohibited.

11.3. An AI answer is not used in an official document, a decision or external correspondence without being checked; the employee is responsible for the result. Decisions affecting people's rights are not made on the basis of an AI result alone.

11.4. AI add-ons and agent apps that require access to the Organisation's systems, email or files are not connected without the permission of the IT unit.

11.5. An employee keeps in mind that voice and video faked with AI may be used for social engineering.

## 12. Social engineering and phone calls
12.1. An employee does not give information, grant access or carry out a request for a person whose identity has not been confirmed.

12.2. Even if a caller presents themselves as a manager, a member of the IT unit, a bank employee or a representative of a state body, the employee:
- does not give a password, one-time code or bank card details;
- does not install remote control software and does not share their screen;
- ends the call and checks the request by calling back on a number taken from an official source.

12.3. An urgent request for a payment, a change of payment details, the sending of confidential information or the granting of access is not carried out until it is confirmed through a second channel, even if it comes in a familiar voice or by video call.

12.4. An employee does not let an outsider follow them into a controlled area and informs the security service about an unaccompanied unknown person.

## 13. Incident reporting
13.1. An employee reports the following:
- a suspicious email, message, call, QR code or person, including a social engineering attempt that failed;
- a password or other data entered on a fake page, or a suspicious file opened;
- the loss or theft of a device, media, access card or document;
- unusual device behaviour (unexpected windows, changed files, a ransom demand);
- data sent to the wrong recipient or disclosed without authorisation;
- a breach of this policy.

13.2. Reports are made through the channels set by the Organisation: the reporting button in email, the reporting function in the cyber hygiene training platform, the IT help desk, or the email or phone of the information security officer. These channels are published on the Organisation's internal portal.

13.3. A report is made immediately after the case is discovered, and no later than 1 hour. If malware is suspected, the device is not switched off; it is disconnected from the network and the employee waits for instructions from the IT unit.

13.4. An employee does not try to investigate the incident on their own, delete traces or contact the attacker.

13.5. No disciplinary measure is applied for a report made in good faith, including when employees report their own mistake. Hiding a mistake or deliberately delaying a report is a breach of this policy.

13.6. The information security officer ensures that information required by law is provided to the competent state bodies.

## 14. Monitoring and privacy
14.1. To protect the security of its information resources, the Organisation may carry out monitoring in accordance with the Constitution of the Republic of Azerbaijan and the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Personal Data".

14.2. Monitoring is limited to system access and activity logs, automated scanning of email for malware and spam, web filtering, the security status of devices, and alerts from data leakage prevention tools.

14.3. Monitoring is proportionate to its lawful purpose. Access to the content of a specific employee's correspondence takes place only for the investigation of an incident or in other cases provided by law, on the written decision of the head of the Organisation, by authorised persons and with documentation.

14.4. Monitoring data is available only to authorised persons, is used only for security purposes and is not kept longer than the period set by the Organisation.

14.5. This policy constitutes notice to the employee about monitoring. An employee may access the personal data collected about them in accordance with the law. To protect the privacy of personal correspondence, an employee does not conduct personal correspondence through work email.

14.6. The Organisation may run phishing simulations. Their results are used to assign additional training and are not on their own a ground for a disciplinary measure.

## 15. Prohibited actions
15.1. Employees are prohibited from:
- using another person's account or handing over their own account to someone else;
- attempting to access information not needed for their duties, or extending their access rights without authorisation;
- bypassing security tools, including web filtering through a VPN, proxy or anonymisation tool;
- scanning the network, searching for vulnerabilities, performing penetration tests, cracking passwords or intercepting traffic without written permission;
- creating, keeping or distributing malware;
- connecting unauthorised devices (Wi-Fi access point, router, mobile modem) to the network;
- using unlicensed software or infringing copyright;
- using the Organisation's resources for cryptocurrency mining, personal business or gambling;
- creating or distributing unlawful, offensive or discriminatory content;
- making unauthorised statements on behalf of the Organisation or impersonating another person;
- copying, removing or disclosing confidential information and personal data without authorisation;
- taking the Organisation's devices and media off the premises without authorisation.

15.2. Security testing carried out by the IT unit and the information security officer within their authority and with the written permission of the head of the Organisation is not a prohibited action.

## 16. Liability
16.1. A breach of this policy may lead to disciplinary liability under the Labour Code of the Republic of Azerbaijan and the Organisation's internal disciplinary rules. Civil servants are also liable under the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Civil Service".

16.2. Disclosure of a state secret, a commercial secret or other information protected by law may be treated as a gross breach of labour duties under the Labour Code. An employee bears material liability under the law for damage caused to the Organisation.

16.3. Unlawful access to a computer system, unlawful interception of computer data, unlawful interference with a system or data, distribution of malware, and breaches of the legislation on personal data and secrets may lead to criminal or administrative liability under the Criminal Code and the Code of Administrative Offences.

16.4. When choosing a measure, the severity of the breach, intent or negligence, the consequences, the employee's previous conduct and whether the incident was reported in time are taken into account. An honest mistake that is reported in time leads first of all to additional training.

## 17. Training and acknowledgement
17.1. An employee reads and acknowledges this policy before obtaining access to information resources.

17.2. An employee completes cyber hygiene training within the period set by the Organisation after joining, and then at least once a year. The Organisation may assign additional training when new threats emerge.

17.3. The policy is published in the Organisation's cyber hygiene training platform. The employee reads the policy, ticks the statement "I have read this policy, I understand it and I will follow it." and signs by entering the password of their account. The platform records the date and time of the acknowledgement and the version of the policy. Each new version of the policy is acknowledged again.

17.4. An acknowledgement made in the platform is an electronic record showing that the employee has read the policy and undertakes to follow it. The Organisation's internal rules may additionally require an electronic signature under the Law of the Republic of Azerbaijan "On Electronic Signature and Electronic Document" or a signature on paper.

17.5. A delay in acknowledgement does not release the employee from duties arising from the law and the Organisation's internal acts.

## 18. Review and update
18.1. The owner of the policy is the information security officer; questions about the policy are addressed to them.

18.2. The policy is reviewed at least once a year, and also when legislation changes, after a serious incident or when a new technology is introduced.

18.3. The policy and any changes to it are approved by the head of the Organisation and enter into force on the day of approval. Each new edition receives a new version number; earlier versions and their acknowledgement records are kept.

18.4. This document is version 1.0 of the policy.
