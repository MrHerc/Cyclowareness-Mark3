## Style rules
Register and address: use formal "siz" in every sentence addressed to the learner (kontrol edin, bildirin, tıklamayın). Never use "sen" unless a lesson character really speaks informally (a scammer posing as a relative, a family member). Buttons and menu commands follow standard Turkish software practice and use the bare verb stem (Başla, Devam et, Gönder, Kaydet, İptal, İzin ver, Bildir). All body text, feedback and AI Sel's speech use siz. Artefacts that pose as a bank, a state body or a company department may use the bureaucratic imperative (tıklayınız, ödeme yapınız, onaylayınız), because that is how real ones read.

Titles: sentence case like az/ru (Oltalama e-postaları), never English Title Case. Short noun phrases or siz-imperatives.

Forms of address: in dialogue, people address colleagues and managers by first name plus Bey/Hanım, capitalised (Kamran Bey, Leyla Hanım). "Mr/Ms + first name" in the English becomes "first name + Bey/Hanım". In written artefacts, "Mr/Ms + surname" becomes "Sayın + surname" (Sayın Hasanov). "Dear user" becomes "Sayın kullanıcı" and "Dear colleagues" becomes "Değerli çalışma arkadaşlarımız".

Gender: Turkish has no grammatical gender, and "o" means he or she, so nothing needs to agree. Do not add gendered words the English does not have.

Quotes: use “…” for every quotation, UI label or quoted email text, and ‘…’ for a quote inside a quote. Never «…» or straight double quotes in visible text (JSON keeps its own ").

Apostrophes: use the ASCII ' before a suffix on a proper name or abbreviation (SMS'te, USB'ye, VPN'e, PIN'inizi, IBAN'ı, MFA'yı, BT'ye, Kamran'ın, Leyla'ya, AI Sel'e, NeyroSöhbət'e). Common nouns never take one (vergi dairesine, mahkemeye). Vowel harmony follows how the abbreviation or name is pronounced. Do not put a suffix on XP, on Cyclowareness or on a multi-word foreign name: rephrase instead (Cyclowareness platformunda, XP kazanın, Nordvale Council adına).

Numbers: a noun after a number stays singular (3 ders, 7 gün, 5 şüpheli mesaj). So in ICU plurals both branches are usually identical: {count, plural, one {# modül} other {# modül}}. Never translate or reorder ICU placeholders ({name}, {xp}, {count}, #). The thousands separator is a period (18.000 manat) and the decimal separator is a comma (12,5). The percent sign comes before the number (%60). Times stay exactly as in the source (09:12), because the checker compares them. Dates read "27 Ekim 2026" or 27.10.2026, with month names capitalised. A bare decimal value in a data field (12.5) must stay exactly as it is, because the parity checker compares it. In spoken "say" text, "18 bin manat" is allowed if it sounds better aloud.

Currency: always manat / AZN after the amount (18.000 manat, 250 AZN). Never convert to TL.

Punctuation: write the ellipsis as the single character …. Prefer a colon or comma over a dash, as the source does. If a dash is needed, use a spaced en dash " – ". No space before ? ! : ;.

Letters: always use ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü correctly. The capital of i is İ and the capital of ı is I (İnternet, İzin ver, İş, İlk, HİZMETE ÖZEL, BİLGİ). Abbreviations stay as they are (IBAN, PIN, SIM). Never type a plain I for a capitalised i-word. Any code that uppercases Turkish text must use toLocaleUpperCase('tr-TR') or an element with lang="tr" for CSS text-transform.

Circumflex: follow the TDK Yazım Kılavuzu. Use it only where TDK writes it, mainly to tell words apart or to mark the relational -î: yapay zekâ, kâğıt, kâr (profit; "kar" is snow), hâlâ (still; "hala" is aunt), resmî, millî, askerî, sahtekâr, imkân, mekân, dükkân, hikâye, rüzgâr. Add it nowhere else (lazım, ilan, kazanç, dikkat stay plain). Prefer "kazanç" over "kâr" in lessons.

Language purity: no Azerbaijani words or spellings in Turkish running text. "məktub, fırıldaq, bildiriş, link, qoşma, brauzer" are wrong; use "e-posta, dolandırıcılık, bildirim, bağlantı, ek, tarayıcı". Avoid unneeded anglicisms: write "bağlantı", not "link", and "parola", not "password". Keep the established ones Türkiye really uses: deepfake, Wi-Fi, VPN, QR kod, CAPTCHA, IBAN, PIN, SIM, XP. In scam artefacts, colloquial forms a real Turkish attacker would use are allowed and even wanted ("linke tıklayınız", "Kameranızı hackledim").

Never translate: Cyclowareness, Safarov Industries, AI Sel, and fictional brand and product names exactly as written, even with ə/x/q (NeyroSöhbət, İşPoçt, BuludXidmət, SahilBank, Nordvale Council …). The same goes for email addresses, domains, URLs, phone numbers, IBANs, card numbers, codes, times, amounts, the currency, ASCII-only file names (Rekvizitler.pdf), brandRef values, the identifiers whatsapp/telegram/generic/chat, and names inside addresses and identifiers (e.mammadov@… stays).

Setting: the stories stay in the fictional Azerbaijani company world (Azerbaijani institutions, manat, +994 numbers). Translate meaning, not words. Artefacts (emails, SMS, chats, calls, screens) must read like a real attack on an employee, in natural Turkish, and keep every red flag and spot phrase the lesson teaches.

Spoken text: "say" text is read aloud by a Turkish voice, so write short spoken sentences that sound natural. Spell character names in their Turkish forms (see names) so the voice says them correctly. Use Turkish fillers: ee, şey, yani, hımm.

## Character names
Rule: Azerbaijani names are written the way Turkish media write them, so a Turkish reader and the Turkish TTS voice say them correctly. ə→e (word-initial Ə→A), q→g but word-final q→k, x→h. c, ç, ş, ğ, ı, ö, ü stay. English spellings (ch, sh, a for ə) go back to these Turkish forms. Fixed press forms: Əliyev→Aliyev, Həsənov→Hasanov. Where the result would look like a misspelling of the same common Turkish name, use the Turkish spelling (Səadət→Saadet). Names inside email addresses and identifiers never change.
