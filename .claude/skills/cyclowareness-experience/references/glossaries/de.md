## Style rules
Address: formal „Sie“ everywhere (Sie, Ihr, Ihnen always capitalised), including AI Sel's tutor lines, feedback and the UI. Attack artefacts (scam SMS, a fake relative on the phone, chats between colleagues) may use „du“ only where the fiction calls for it (a relative, a close colleague in a chat); the narrator and every instruction to the learner stay „Sie“. Instructions use the Sie imperative („Prüfen Sie die Adresse.“). Buttons and actions use the infinitive („Phishing melden“, „Link öffnen“, „Löschen“, „Weiter“). Avoid a subtitle or sentence that starts with „Sie“ meaning „she/it“, because readers take it as the formal „you“ (write „Die Falle will …“ instead).
Gender: gender-inclusive only where it reads naturally. Prefer neutral forms (Mitarbeitende, Führungskräfte, Beschäftigte, Nutzende, Kriminelle, Staatsanwaltschaft, Personalabteilung, Anlageberatung). Salutations use pairs („Liebe Kolleginnen und Kollegen“). Never use a gender star, colon, underscore or Binnen-I. A real character takes their own gender (die Kollegin Aynur, der Ermittler Elçin Həsənov, die Stellvertreterin Səadət). The BSI-style generic „Angreifer“, „Absender“ and „Empfänger“ are acceptable.
Register: plain, calm, professional corporate e-learning German (BSI tone). Short sentences that sound natural read aloud, because the `say` lines are narrated by TTS. No marketing hype and no Austrian or Swiss regionalisms. Prefer established German terms. Keep anglicisms only where German corporate usage really has them (Phishing, Social Engineering, Deepfake, Ransomware, Infostealer, Update, Backup, Link, App, Browser, Meeting, Fake-Shop, Fake News, Clean Desk, Cookie, Autofill). „Sitzung“ only in a clearly technical sign-in context, because it normally means a meeting.
Quotes: „…“ (U+201E / U+201C); nested quotes ‚…‘. UI labels and button names are quoted the same way („Phishing melden“). The apostrophe is ’.
Punctuation: the dash is a spaced en dash („ – “). The ellipsis is „…“. After a colon, capitalise only when a full independent sentence follows („Kein Passwort nötig: Eine gestohlene Sitzung reicht“, „Vor dem Senden: was, an wen und wie?“). Titles take no final full stop, except a deliberate one-word stop such as „Stopp.“. Question titles end with „?“. Do not join two main clauses with a bare comma in a title; use „ – “ instead.
Spelling: standard German orthography (Duden) for Germany, with ß (Bußgeld, außen, schließen, groß), also for readers in Austria and Switzerland. Hyphenate compounds that contain an abbreviation, a foreign word or a number (QR-Code, USB-Stick, E-Mail, KI-Tools, Phishing-Mail, Passwort-Manager, Software-Update, 2FA-Code, SIM-Swapping, Fake-Shop, İşPoçt-Administrator). Titles use normal German capitalisation: the first word and nouns are capitalised, never English title case.
Numbers: decimal comma (1,20 AZN; 23,40 AZN), dot as the thousands separator (18.000 Manat), percent with a no-break space („98 %“). Times stay as written (09:12); in a sentence write „um 10:00 Uhr“. Dates in body text: „27. Oktober 2026“ or „27.10.2026“, with German weekday and month names. Amounts keep the same value and currency: „AZN“ as the code, „Manat“ in running text (der Manat, Pl. Manat). Exception from the lesson checker: a whole value that is only a bare decimal such as `12.5` stays unchanged; decimals inside a sentence take the comma.
Script and letters: Latin script with ä ö ü ß. Azerbaijani names and places keep their Azerbaijani letters (ə ç ş ğ ı İ ö ü) in the text, exactly as the matching .az.json lesson writes them. Addresses and identifiers stay unchanged.
Never translate: AI Sel (never „KI Sel“), Cyclowareness, Safarov Industries. Fictional brand and product names keep their native brands.json spelling (İşPoçt even where the English writes „IshPocht“, BuludXidmət, NeyroSöhbət, UlduzBank, ÇevikKargo, DocuSfera, ParolSeyf, SənədAxını …). Also unchanged: e-mail addresses, domains and URLs (also inside sentences and in „Name <address>“ lines), phone numbers, IBANs, card numbers, codes, times, amounts and currency, Wi-Fi network names and hotspot brand names (Kafe_Nargiz, „Kafe Nargiz Wi-Fi“), ICU placeholders ({name}, {count, plural …}: translate the words inside, keep the syntax, German plural categories one/other), JSON keys, ids, the spot/highlight/brandRef/href/kind/outcome values and the app identifiers whatsapp/telegram/generic/chat. File names made only of ASCII letters, digits, hyphens and dots (Rekvizitler.pdf, Sened.pdf.exe) stay, and by choice all other attachment file names stay too, so the trick extensions remain identical. Descriptive institution and department names that the English translates are translated into German (Central Investigation Bureau → Zentrales Ermittlungsbüro, Tax Control Centre → Steueraufsichtszentrum, Capital Justice Court → Hauptstädtisches Gericht, IT Support → IT-Support, HR department → Personalabteilung). Real place names use the German exonym where one exists (Baku, Aserbaidschan, Wien, Vereinigtes Königreich), otherwise the Azerbaijani spelling (Gəncə, Nərimanov, Xəzər).
MARKET: translate in the Azerbaijani world exactly as this glossary says, then run `npx tsx scripts/apply-market.ts de` from portal/. It rewrites the de lesson files with content/market/de.json (local names, cities, money, phone numbers, authorities, brands and the matching addresses), and `scripts/check-lesson-locales.ts` fails a de lesson that still holds a token of the Azerbaijani world. Never localise by hand: the pass reads the Azerbaijani spellings below.
Setting (translation input): the stories stay in the fictional Azerbaijani company world. Do not move them to Germany and do not replace AZN with euros. Do not use German state classification levels (VS-NfD and the like). Realistic German-language attack wording is fine (an SMS in German pretending to be ÇevikKargo), but keep every red flag the English teaches.
UI: always WLAN (never „Wi-Fi“ in running text), KI for AI, Lektion / Modul / Lernpfad / Übungslabor, Lernserie, Serienschutz, Tagesziel, Liga, XP, Stufe, Zertifikat, Risikowert. The player's swipe labels are „Falle“ / „Sicher“. Lesson titles are exactly the values in content/library/i18n.de.json.
