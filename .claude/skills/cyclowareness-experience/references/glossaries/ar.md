## Style rules
REGISTER: Modern Standard Arabic as Saudi corporate and government digital services write it (NCA, SDAIA, Absher, Tawakkalna, Nafath): clear, calm, friendly-professional, short sentences. No dialect words, no Egyptian/Levantine administrative terms (وكيل النيابة, تكنولوجيا), no flowery literary style. Prefer Saudi lexical choices: الجوال، الجهة، اضغط، الدعم الفني، تقنية المعلومات، البيانات البنكية، رقم الآيبان، كشف الراتب. Anglicisms only where Saudi usage really has them, written in Latin: Wi-Fi, USB, QR, SMS, VPN, CAPTCHA, SIM, PIN, IBAN, OTP, MFA, XP. A term from the glossary is used every time for its concept (one concept, one term). Words that can be misread in a short title get a qualifier (مدير كلمات المرور, not المدير; مسؤولي الأنظمة, not المسؤولين; القيادة alone reads as driving in Saudi usage, so leaders are القيادات or كبار المسؤولين).
FORM OF ADDRESS: speak to the learner directly in the second person SINGULAR (أنت، ـك). Never the plural أنتم as politeness, never third-person "the user" in instructions.
GENDER RULE (apply everywhere): (1) Use a neutral construction whenever it reads naturally. Lesson titles, buttons, labels, tabs and menu items are verbal nouns or noun phrases (تسجيل الدخول، الإبلاغ عن تصيد، إيقاف الانتشار، المشاركة بذكاء). Unvocalized forms written the same for both genders count as neutral: the possessive suffix ـك (حسابك، كلمة مرورك) and the past-tense ـت (ضغطت، فقدت، عملت). Impersonal and passive phrasings are neutral too (ما لا يجوز لصقه، عند البيع). (2) When a direct verb is needed (imperatives, present-tense "you", feedback such as أحسنت), use the generic MASCULINE singular, unvocalized, as Saudi government digital services do (تحقق، أبلغ، لا تدفع، احتفظ). (3) Never use slash or bracket forms (أبلغ/ي، ـت(ِ)، عزيزي/عزيزتي). (4) Story characters keep their own gender: a female character uses feminine forms about herself (أنا متأكدة), lines addressed to her inside a dialogue use the feminine (أين أنتِ يا آيسل؟), and nouns for her role are feminine (نرمين، زميلتك). Every line addressed to the learner stays generic masculine, whoever speaks it (Elvin to the learner: «هل تسمعني؟»). (5) The learner's own lines ("me", "You" in a dialogue) are first person: prefer verbs, which are neutral (لا أعرف، سأتصل بك لاحقًا), over gendered adjectives or participles (avoid لست متأكدًا); if one is unavoidable, use the masculine.
PUNCTUATION: Arabic comma ، semicolon ؛ question mark ؟ in Arabic text. Period, colon, exclamation mark and ellipsis … are the normal glyphs. No space before punctuation, one space after. Latin punctuation stays inside Latin strings (addresses, URLs, file names).
QUOTES: «…» for every quotation, quoted UI label, quoted speech and title (فخ «السماح»، مكالمة «الدعم الفني»، فخاخ «تمكين التحرير»). Nested quotes use "…". Never ASCII " in visible Arabic text. In messages/ar, the player's `quote` pattern is «{text}», not “{text}”.
NUMBERS (decided): Western digits 0-9 only, never Arabic-Indic ٠-٩. Decimal point "." (a bare value such as 12.5 stays exactly as in the source). Thousands separator "," as in the source (18,000). Percent: 98% with the Latin sign. Times 24-hour as in the source (09:12، 20:00). Phone numbers, codes, IBANs, card numbers, amounts and versions are copied verbatim. Small counts in running text may be words (ستة أرقام) where the English uses words. Arabic noun-number agreement must be correct (دقيقة واحدة، دقيقتان، 3 دقائق، 15 دقيقة، 100 رسالة). ICU messages use the full Arabic plural set: zero, one, two, few, many, other.
CURRENCY: the amount stays as it is and "manat" becomes مانات after the number (18,000 مانات، 600 مانات). Where the source writes AZN, keep AZN in Latin after the number (250 AZN). Never convert to riyals.
DATES: Gregorian calendar only (no Hijri). Months يناير فبراير مارس أبريل مايو يونيو يوليو أغسطس سبتمبر أكتوبر نوفمبر ديسمبر (the set used in Saudi Arabia, not كانون/شباط). Format: 27 أكتوبر 2026. Weekdays: الأحد الاثنين الثلاثاء الأربعاء الخميس الجمعة السبت.
SCRIPT AND TYPOGRAPHY: no tashkeel. One standing exception: tanween fath, written on the letter before the alif (دائمًا، أولًا، أيضًا، دليلًا). A damma or shadda is added only where a word would otherwise be misread, typically a first-person past verb that could be read as "you" (رسائل «اخترقتُ كاميرتك»). Spell hamza correctly (إ أ ؤ ئ ء). Keep ة distinct from ه and ى distinct from ي (ليلى، على vs علي). No tatweel (kashida). Never put the Arabic article on a Latin word: put an Arabic head noun before it (شبكة Wi-Fi، رمز QR، شريحة SIM، اختبار CAPTCHA، أجهزة تخزين USB). Do not insert LRM/RLM or other bidi control characters: the UI isolates Latin runs.
MARKET: translate in the Azerbaijani world exactly as this glossary says, then run `npx tsx scripts/apply-market.ts ar` from portal/. It rewrites the ar lesson files with content/market/ar.json (local names, cities, money, phone numbers, authorities, brands and the matching addresses), and `scripts/check-lesson-locales.ts` fails a ar lesson that still holds a token of the Azerbaijani world. Never localise by hand: the pass reads the Azerbaijani spellings below.
STORY WORLD (translation input): the stories stay in the fictional Azerbaijani company world. Authorities are translated generically (النيابة العامة، المدعي العام، المحكمة، مصلحة الضرائب، الشرطة). Never swap in Saudi bodies or services (هيئة الزكاة والضريبة والجمارك، أبشر، نفاذ، NCA) or Saudi classification levels. Real places use their Arabic names: باكو، كنجة، أذربيجان، فيينا، أمستردام، المملكة المتحدة، حي نريمانوف. Fictional towns and organisations stay Latin.
NARRATION: `say` is natural spoken MSA that sounds good read aloud by Arabic TTS. `spoken` keeps every delivery tag (<breath>, <short pause>, <sigh>, <throat-clearing> …) in a natural place. Arabic hesitation fillers: «أمم»، «آه»، «يعني» (sparingly).
NEVER TRANSLATE: Cyclowareness, Safarov Industries, AI Sel (always Latin, also inside Arabic sentences: «اسأل AI Sel»). Fictional brand and product names exactly as the English source of that lesson spells them (NeyroSöhbət, İşPoçt and IshPocht, BuludXidmet, DocuSfera, Kontakto, SahilBank, Laman Handmade, Cafe_Nargiz/Kafe_Nargiz, Nargiz Market, Alov Servis/AlovServis, Khazar Logistics, Nordvale, Nordhaven …): do not unify spellings that differ between lessons. Email addresses, domains, URLs, ASCII file names, phone numbers, IBANs, card numbers, codes, times, amounts. JSON keys, ids, spot/kind/pose values and the identifiers whatsapp, telegram, generic, chat. People's names keep their Latin form inside addresses and identifiers (leyla.hasanova@…) and are written in Arabic script in running text and in speaker/name fields (names table). Lesson and module titles are exactly the values in content/library/i18n.ar.json.

## Character names
RULE: names of Arabic origin take their established Arabic spelling (علي، حسن، حسين، رشاد، رؤوف، كمال، فؤاد، توفيق، فريد، سمير، ناظم، كنعان، رنا). Persian-origin names with an established Arabic-script form keep it (كامران، جاويد، نريمان). Other names are spelled by sound: c/j→ج, ç/ch→تش, ş/sh→ش, x/kh→خ, Latin g→غ (the Arabic-press convention for Turkic names), Azerbaijani q→ق, v→ف, ə/a→ا or nothing, ü/u/ö/o→و, e/i→ي; a final -a/-ə of a female name is ا, never ة (سابينا، إلنارا، نرمينا). Surname endings: -ov→ـوف, -ova→ـوفا, -yev→ـييف, -yeva→ـييفا, -li/-lı→ـلي, -zadə/-zada→ زاده (separate word). One person has one spelling everywhere, whatever the source spelling: Guliyev = Quliyev, Mammadov = Məmmədov, Elchin = Elçin, Narmin = Nərmin.
